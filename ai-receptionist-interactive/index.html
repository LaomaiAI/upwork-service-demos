One receptionist, four kinds of business

Clinics and hospitals

The problem: missed calls are lost patients - about a third of calls come after hours, and most callers who reach voicemail just call a competitor. What the receptionist does: answers every call in seconds, books, reschedules and follows up. Try Scenario A ▶

Law firms

The problem: a missed intake call is a client who hires another firm, and every new matter needs details captured correctly. What the receptionist does: opens an intake record, notes the matter and deadlines, runs a conflict check and books the consultation. Try Scenario D ▶

E-commerce stores

The problem: shoppers with a shipping, returns or stock question who get no answer buy somewhere else. What the receptionist does: answers from the store's own policies and prices, reserves stock and takes the order. Try Scenario E ▶

Manufacturing companies

The problem: quote requests and distributor enquiries arrive by phone at all hours and sit in voicemail until someone gets back. What the receptionist does: captures the enquiry, the product and the quantity, logs it for the sales team and calls the enquirer back with a quote slot. See how follow-up works ▶

More than answering the phone

1

Records every caller

Every call leaves a record: name, number, what they wanted and what happened. The front desk sees it all the next morning.

Sample record: Emma Johnson - 512-555-0148 - new patient, cleaning - booked Tue 10:00 AM - SMS sent.

2

Calls back and follows up

Missed a call while the line was busy? The receptionist calls the person back, books them, and reminds them the day before.

Sample: missed call 6:42 PM - called back 6:43 PM - patient booked. Reminder text scheduled for the day before, 9:00 AM.

3

After-sales care

Two days after a visit or a delivery, the receptionist checks in by text or call, and books a follow-up if the customer needs one.

Sample text: "Hi Emma, this is Bright Smile Dental checking in. How is everything after your cleaning on Tuesday? Reply if you need anything."

4

Pre-sales answers

Shipping, returns, stock, prices - answered from the business's own information, not guessed. When the caller is ready, the receptionist takes the order or books the next step.

See it: Scenario E - the store assistant answers policy questions and creates order #NW-2481.
