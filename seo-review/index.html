1

Technical foundation

Goal: make the store fast, mobile-friendly, and easy for Google to crawl and index. Nothing else works until this is solid.

How to do it

  1. Crawl first, fix errors first. Run a full crawl and clear status errors: 404s get 301 redirects to the right page, redirect chains collapse to a single hop.
  2. Speed: compress images to WebP, lazy-load below-fold images, defer chat and review widgets. Target Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5s on product pages.
  3. Mobile: check tap targets, font sizes and layout on a real phone. No horizontal scroll, no text cut off.
  4. Indexation: a clean XML sitemap listing every indexable page, and canonical rules so filtered and sorted URLs do not create duplicate pages.
  5. International: hreflang for each market version (en-US / en-GB / de-DE) plus x-default, so shoppers see the right currency and shipping.

2

Keyword research

Goal: pick the keywords that sell, not just the keywords that are popular.

How to do it

  1. Start from the category, not the product. List what shoppers type before they know your brand — “linen duvet cover set”, not “Nordhaus linen set”.
  2. Sort by search intent: informational (learning), commercial (comparing), transactional (ready to buy). Commercial terms pay first.
  3. Be honest about difficulty. A new store wins long-tail and comparison terms first; head terms come later, after authority builds.
  4. Map one keyword to one page. One keyword, one page, one job. Two pages chasing the same term cannibalise each other.
  5. Check the competitor gap: which page-one terms do rivals hold that the store does not? Those are the shopping list.

GEO — also check the AI engines. For each core question, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews how they answer it, and note who gets cited. If the AI answers quote comparison guides and review roundups, those are the formats to produce. If they quote a competitor’s buying guide, that guide is the bar to beat.

This is a manual check, done once per category and repeated monthly — not a dashboard, and not scraped data.

3

On-page optimisation

Goal: every page tells Google exactly what it is about, in the words shoppers use.

How to do it

  1. Title tags: target keyword near the front, brand at the end, under 60 characters. One page, one title, no duplicates.
  2. Meta descriptions: one sentence — benefit plus keyword — written to earn the click, not to stuff keywords.
  3. Product pages: unique title and description, one H1, alt text on every image, Product schema with price and availability.
  4. Collection pages: 150–300 words of useful copy above or below the grid, so there is something to rank besides the product grid.
  5. Internal links: every new page links to its collection and two products; orphan pages get linked in from relevant content.

4

Content plan: blog & buying guides

Goal: publish the pages Google can rank and shoppers actually read — then keep publishing on a calendar.

How to do it

  1. Commercial first: buying guides and comparisons that lead to a product. These earn money, not just traffic.
  2. Informational second: care guides and room ideas that earn links and build topical authority.
  3. One question per piece, answered in the first paragraph. One keyword per piece, from the Step 2 map.
  4. Publish on a calendar: two pieces a month to start, same weekdays, no gaps. Consistency beats bursts.
  5. Every piece links to its collection and two product pages, so content both ranks and sells.

GEO — write so AI can quote you. AI answers lift text that is easy to lift: lead with a direct 40–60 word answer, then a comparison table or a short list, then a FAQ. Add FAQPage and HowTo structured data so engines can use the answer verbatim. A page that buries its answer in paragraph five does not get cited.

5

Off-page authority

Goal: earn the links and mentions that tell Google the store is trusted.

How to do it

  1. Digital PR: one data-led or seasonal story per quarter — a small survey, a trend roundup, a gift guide journalists can actually use.
  2. Get listed where makers get listed: supplier and stockist pages, design directories, gift guides.
  3. Collect reviews: on-site product reviews plus independent review platforms. Real reviews feed both rankings and AI answers.
  4. Promote every guide where the audience already is: Pinterest, home forums, newsletters. A guide nobody sees earns no links.
  5. Never buy links. One real mention beats ten paid ones, and paid links are the fastest way to lose rankings.

6

GEO: Generative Engine Optimisation AI SEARCH

Goal: get the store cited and recommended inside AI answers — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews — not just ranked on Google.

How to do it

  1. Add llms.txt: a plain-text file at /llms.txt that tells AI crawlers what the store sells, its key pages, shipping and returns, in short factual lines. No marketing fluff — crawlers quote facts.
  2. Structured data everywhere it applies: Product, FAQPage, HowTo, Review and Organization schema, so engines can read the facts instead of guessing them.
  3. Be citable: publish pages with numbers, comparisons and named products, and keep facts current. AI engines quote sources that state facts clearly; vague pages never get cited.
  4. Earn brand mentions, not just links: AI answers lean on reviews, forums, videos and roundups. Get the store mentioned there — review platforms, home forums, YouTube demos — with the same store name and positioning every time.
  5. Keep the brand story consistent: same name, same positioning, same key facts everywhere an AI might read. Contradictions get the store dropped from answers.

7

Measure & iterate

Goal: know what moved, then do more of it — on Google and in AI answers.

How to do it

  1. Search Console, weekly: clicks, impressions, average position, and any new coverage errors. Fix errors the week they appear.
  2. Rank tracking, weekly: the keyword list from Step 2, checked every week, same terms every time.
  3. Monthly review: organic sessions, revenue from organic, and the keyword gap table, updated. What improved, what stalled, what changes next.
  4. AI visibility, monthly: repeat the 10-question citation check from Step 6 and log AI-referred sessions separately from Google traffic.
  5. Change one thing at a time per page, so you know what worked. A page that changes five things at once teaches nothing.
